**Summary:**
Delaying the laminar-to-turbulent transition of flow over an aircraft has significant potential for reducing its climate impact. A robust passive flow control method is an attractive way to achieve this transition delay. The current work is focused on designing such a passive flow control mechanism that is based on Local Resonance Units (LRUs). In particular, this mechanism is explored to attenuate the Tollmien–Schlichting (TS) waves in a flat-plate boundary layer that drive its transition to turbulence. A foundational principle underlying the design of LRUs is a previously identified mechanism whereby modifying the local surface admittance was shown to influence the amplitude of a TS wave through wave interference.

 Design criteria for the surface admittance required to achieve TS wave attenuation were obtained from the literature. This thesis is set to design an LRU that can meet these design criteria. Particularly, these criteria included the favourable limits of the admittance phase through which a TS wave can be attenuated and a subwavelength resonance requirement, together with an operational frequency requirement. Drawing inspiration from research on acoustic wave control using metamaterials, composite resonators are chosen as an LRU concept that could meet the above criteria for TS wave attenuation. Two different types of composite resonators: one incorporating a single rigid insert and the other incorporating dual rigid inserts were investigated. The dual-insert composite resonator allowed us to explore the admittance phase behaviour across the coupled resonance phenomena observed in systems with multiple degrees of freedom.
\par  The admittance response of the composite resonators was explored through numerical and theoretical investigations. The numerical investigations involved frequency-domain simulations of harmonic pressure load analysis of the resonators. Firstly, the admittance phase behaviour of the resonators across different resonance modes was explored. These investigations revealed that the response phase of composite resonators was much like that of previously explored resonator concepts, showing a fundamental similarity in resonance mechanisms. Adding a degree of freedom did not yield the desired admittance phase close to resonance. The resonators showed a favourable admittance phase for TS-wave attenuation only at frequencies away from the resonance frequencies. A theoretical investigation based on a 1D energy-transfer analysis provided a stronger conclusion about the possible admittance phase values. This revealed that the admittance response of any passive single-input, single-output (SISO) model, such as those explored here, can only offer a limited range of admittance phase. This limited the design space of admittance phase suitable for TS wave attenuation. The composite resonators had to be designed to operate at post-resonance conditions within this limited admittance-phase design space. This came at the cost of reduced admittance amplitude.
  Further parametric studies were conducted to tune the composite resonators to operate at the desired conditions with a target design phase at the post-resonance conditions. These investigations revealed that material density, together with stiffness, limits the admittance amplitude. Furthermore, it was found that the addition of a degree of freedom does not prove to be advantageous in terms of admittance amplitude either. 
 Harmonic-Navier-Stokes simulations were conducted to model the interaction of the tuned composite resonators with a TS wave through an admittance boundary condition. Using composite resonators designed to operate at post-resonance frequencies resulted in TS wave attenuation; however, post-resonance operation penalties and constraints on admittance amplitude meant that only very small attenuation could be achieved in an air-based boundary layer. It was shown that, as expected, by changing the flow medium to water, a significantly higher TS wave attenuation can be achieved. This is a consequence of matching the inertial properties of the fluid and the control element.
